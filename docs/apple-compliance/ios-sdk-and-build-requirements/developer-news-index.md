## Tax and price updates for apps, In-App Purchases, and subscriptions

November 17, 2026

The App Store is designed to make it easy to sell your digital goods and services globally, with support for 43 currencies across 175 storefronts.

From time to time, we may need to adjust prices or your proceeds due to changes in tax regulations or foreign exchange rates. These adjustments are made using publicly available exchange rate information from reputable financial media (e.g., The Wall Street Journal) or financial companies (e.g., Bloomberg) to help make sure prices for apps and In-App Purchases stay consistent across all storefronts.

**Tax updates**

Your proceeds from the sale of eligible apps and In‑App Purchases have been modified in:

- Botswana: Value‑added tax (VAT) introduction of 14%
- Cameroon: Significant Economic Presence (SEP) tax introduction of 3%
- Côte d'Ivoire: SEP tax introduction of 3%
- Rwanda: VAT introduction of 18%

Exhibit B of the Paid Applications Agreement will be updated to indicate that Apple collects and remits applicable taxes in Botswana and Rwanda.¹

In addition, a new “Software as a Service (SaaS)” attribute is now available for the following tax categories: App Store Software, Software Training Material, Dating, and Boosting. If this tax attribute is relevant to your apps or In-App Purchases, you can review and update your selections in the Pricing and Availability section of Apps.

Moreover, effective January 1, 2027, the following categories of apps will be subject to sales and use tax in California: SaaS, App Store Software, Software Training Material, Dating, and Boosting. Prices on the United States storefront are displayed to customers exclusive of taxes, so the tax change will not affect them. Your proceeds will also not change. The total amount customers are charged, inclusive of tax, may increase.

**Price updates (effective December 3, 2026)**

Pricing for apps and In-App Purchases will be updated for Botswana, Colombia, Egypt, Rwanda, and Türkiye if you haven’t selected one of these storefronts as the base storefront for your app or In‑App Purchase.² These updates also consider the VAT introductions listed in the tax updates section above.

If you’ve selected Botswana, Colombia, Egypt, Rwanda, or Türkiye as the base storefront for your app or In-App Purchase, prices on that storefront won’t change. On other storefronts, prices will be updated to maintain equalization with your chosen base price.

Prices won’t change in any region if your In‑App Purchase is an auto‑renewable subscription.

Prices won’t change on the storefronts where you manually manage prices instead of using the automated equalized prices.

The Pricing and Availability section of Apps has been updated in [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/login/) to display these upcoming price changes. As always, you can change the prices of your apps, In‑App Purchases, and auto‑renewable subscriptions at any time.

## Updates to Device Control and Data Access, and Full Disk Access in macOS

November 16, 2026

For decades, Mac computers have been loved by creators and developers who build, automate, and innovate using cutting-edge technologies and tools. We want to keep empowering our community in these pursuits, while helping our users stay safe and in control of their data and privacy. In macOS 27.3 beta 2 today, we’re introducing increased transparency and more granular options for two of the APIs: Device Control and Data Access, and Full Disk Access. Before granting an app such extraordinary access, users should understand that it puts all of the data on their Mac at risk. These changes are designed to help them do that and to empower them to make informed decisions about their own data and privacy. The changes include:

- A split of Device Control and Data Access into two settings: (1) System & App Protection Override, and (2) Accessibility

&nbsp;

- A single method for granting permissions by selecting the requesting app from a list

&nbsp;

- Guidance to encourage users to grant access to just the specific apps (for System & App Protection Override) or folders (for Full Disk Access) that are required for their workflows

&nbsp;

- Increased visibility into the wide range of data apps will receive access to or control over if full permissions are granted

&nbsp;

- The option for users to configure apps to ask every time they require access

&nbsp;

- A transparency indicator when a requesting app is controlling another app using System & App Protection Override

Developers should be prepared to adjust how they onboard users and make sure their apps can gracefully handle having access to only a limited set of folders or apps when utilizing these APIs. Users will see these updates when approving new app permission requests. All app permissions previously granted by users will remain in effect.

Developers of apps that use Full Disk Access for disk backup, or the new Accessibility settings to provide assistive technologies, can request entitlements to deliver the same experience for new installations that they offered before macOS 27.3. Currently, known apps aren’t affected and will keep the same experience without an entitlement. Additional entitlement details will be available soon.

## Siri AI is now open to all developers

November 3, 2026

![A Siri AI app icon on a dark gray rounded square, showing a sphere that shades from dark gray at the top to light gray at the bottom, with a ribbon of iridescent light curving across its middle.](https://devimages-cdn.apple.com/wwdc-services/articles/images/511D5BB8-613D-4B6E-BB7A-C19C1991B592/2048.jpeg)

Starting today with the iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, macOS 27.3, and visionOS 27.3 betas, a new Developer Preview enables any developer to integrate their app with Siri AI, giving businesses and developers new ways to reach and serve their customers.

Siri AI, an entirely new version of Siri that is profoundly more capable, conversational, and personal, is designed to be deeply integrated into Apple’s operating systems and the apps people use every day, including the apps from our vast ecosystem of developers. Thousands of developers have already built integrations with Siri to let users ask for directions, manage a to-do list, send a message, or catch up on an audiobook. Siri AI is available in many languages, including English, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish and will soon expand to support Traditional Chinese, Norwegian, Turkish, and Vietnamese. People around the world are turning to Siri AI to get things done every day, which means more customers can discover and use apps just by asking.

Today’s preview allows any app to bring its content and actions directly into Siri AI by using App Intents, making it easier for all apps to tap into Siri AI’s newest capabilities, including personal context understanding, onscreen awareness, natural language, and more. So people can just ask for help in their own words, and Siri AI carries out any custom action you’ve built, from checking a delivery to booking a reservation to placing an order. For complex requests, we’re also introducing skills to let you teach Siri AI how to combine several actions into a single experience. And Siri AI can now surface any entity in the Spotlight semantic index, not just the ones that fit an existing schema.

We started rolling out these new capabilities across many Apple apps, including Calendar, Files, Finder, Journal, Mail, Maps, Messages, Music, Notes, Photos, Preview, Reminders, Safari, Shortcuts, and Weather, so users can do things like find a coffee shop they visited last week or archive files that haven’t been opened in over a year. There are many more actions available now as examples for you to reference. To see what you can do in each app, just ask Siri AI.

This developer beta offers a sneak peek at Siri AI app intents for developers. With this early Developer Preview, your app can work directly with Siri AI, giving you a first look at new ways to reach and serve your customers. This preview provides early access before first shipping to customers in a few months; and with submission to the App Store opening up in the spring, developers have time to explore, build, and help shape their experiences before launch. Try it out and share your feedback.

As you explore early, we will follow up with more details about the terms and usage policies for experiences enabled through Siri AI. More to share soon.

To learn more about the new APIs, how to use them, and how to evaluate your app’s experience with Siri AI, read new [documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/siri-ai-developer-preview).

It is available in English, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish. Some features may not be available in all regions or languages.

&nbsp;

- Siri AI is currently unavailable in the EU in iOS, iPadOS, and watchOS. Apple is working constructively with the EU in an attempt to find a path forward.

&nbsp;

- Siri AI is not available for users under 13.

&nbsp;

Daily limits may vary by feature, request complexity, system demand, system policies, and other factors. Expanded access to such features will be available for a fee in the future. Use of these features is subject to the Apple Intelligence terms and conditions. Learn more at support.apple.com/148587.

## Upcoming: New age rating content descriptors in App Store Connect

November 2, 2026

Starting November 16, 2026, App Store Connect will include new content descriptors to help ensure your app’s age ratings accurately reflect its features and are in accordance with local regulations.

When completing the age rating questionnaire in App Store Connect, you’ll need to declare whether your app includes:

- **Shared Location:** Sharing of real-world location with another person or group.
- **Dating and Relationships:** Features that enable dating or relationship connections between real people and/or a virtual companion that engages a user in sexual or romantic content.

You must provide these declarations when submitting an app update or making changes to your age rating questionnaire. All apps must have these declarations completed by January 12, 2027.

Apps that include Dating and Relationships receive a minimum age rating of 18+. Apps that include Shared Location may not have an impact on their minimum age rating in some regions.

In Brazil, local regulations require an A16 rating for Shared Location and an A18 rating for Dating and Relationships. If you believe your app qualifies for a lower age rating in Brazil — for example, if your app provides location sharing specifically designed for parents and children — you can [request an official rating](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/seus-direitos/classificacao-1) directly from Brazil’s [Ministry of Justice and Public Security](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/seus-direitos/classificacao-1/paginas-classificacao-indicativa/guia-de-classificacao) (MJSP/Ministério da Justiça e Segurança Pública). Once verified, the official rating will display on the App Store in Brazil.

[Learn more about Brazil age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions#brazil-age-rating-values)

## Strengthening App Review Guidelines against nonconsensual intimate imagery and bait-and-switch apps

October 16, 2026

The App Review Guidelines are a core part of the safe and trusted experience that the App Store strives to offer users around the world — and the foundation of that trust is clear standards that are consistently enforced.

As outlined in our App Review Guidelines, pornographic material has long been prohibited on the App Store — this includes apps that generate nudity or sexually explicit imagery, such as “nudification” apps that create sexually explicit images of real individuals without their consent. Apps that host user-generated content are required to filter objectionable material, give users a way to report offensive content, and let users block abusive accounts. Apps containing hidden or undocumented features are also prohibited on the App Store, including apps that misrepresent their purpose during review and only reveal harmful capabilities — such as “nudification” — after approval, a tactic commonly known as bait-and-switch.

We want great apps and the innovative developers behind them to succeed on the App Store, and as new risks and novel user experiences emerge, we evolve our App Review Guidelines to maintain the trust of users and developers alike. Today we’re publishing updates that strengthen and extend the existing App Store protections, continuing to build on our long-held standards for privacy, quality, and safety.

For complete details, see the [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

### Changes

**1.2:** Content moderation requirements now explicitly cover nonconsensual intimate imagery — including imagery generated using AI tools — as content that apps must proactively prevent and remove from their platforms. Developers who fail to implement adequate safeguards — or don’t remedy violations after notice — risk violating the Developer Code of Conduct and being removed from the App Store.

**2.5.19:** This rule clarifies our longstanding prohibition on apps misrepresenting what they do during the App Review process and then changing after approval — for example, adding features or functionality that weren’t included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review. Apps found engaging in this conduct are subject to removal from the App Store and removal from the Apple Developer Program.

**Section 5:** The App Store has always prohibited apps that facilitate human trafficking or the exploitation of children (including child sexual abuse material). When we find instances of apps containing such content, we remove them from the App Store. We’ve updated Section 5 of the App Review guidelines to make clear that apps with such content will be removed from the App Store and blocked from alternative distribution.

We’re committed to keeping bad actors off the App Store, with established processes in place to review and act on apps that violate our guidelines. Last year, we prevented over 150K apps with safety issues from reaching customers and terminated over 193K developer accounts for engaging in fraud and abuse. These violations trace back to malicious actors — in some cases coordinated fraud operations — working to conceal harmful content that only becomes active once an app is approved, and as their tactics evolve, we continue to evolve our own tools and resources to prevent and catch this kind of behavior even faster. We do not hesitate to remove apps, and terminate the developers behind them, when they violate these guidelines.

## Updates to Full Disk Access in macOS

October 2, 2026

We give developers powerful APIs to build incredible capabilities into their apps for Apple products, backed by a set of controls designed to protect users’ private data. Full Disk Access largely sidesteps these controls in order to allow backup apps to function properly on the Mac. Some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing everything on their systems—including files, mail, messages, and even browsing history—without users’ full knowledge and understanding. For communication apps, this can also compromise the privacy of the people users are communicating with.

Going forward, we will introduce additional controls to ensure that users who genuinely wish to grant an app this extraordinary level of access can only do so with very explicit user action. Addressing this is critical. As AI agents become increasingly capable and autonomous, the risks associated with this level of access will grow substantially. We are committed to ensuring users clearly understand these risks before granting such access, so they can make informed decisions about their own data and privacy.

## Update for Apps Distributed in Texas

June 3, 2026

Due to a recent court ruling lifting an injunction on Texas law SB 2420, new Apple Accounts in Texas are now subject to the law, which introduced age assurance requirements for app marketplaces and developers. As previously [announced](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=2ezb6jhj), this includes age assurance and parent or guardian consent on behalf of minors under the age of 18 for downloads, Apple In-App Purchases, and significant changes associated with an app. Parents or guardians will also be able to revoke their consent for any app they previously approved for their child. These changes will go into effect starting June 4, 2026.

Developers can request age category data for these Apple Accounts through the [Declared Age Range API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/declaredagerange/). For significant changes, developers should use the [Significant Change API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/PermissionKit/SignificantAppUpdateTopic/) under the PermissionKit framework. As a reminder, it’s the developer’s responsibility to determine when there’s a significant change to their app. To learn about a parent or guardian’s revocation of consent, the App Store is providing a server notification that developers can configure to receive notifications that consent has been withdrawn for their app on a child or teen’s device.

**Next steps**

1.  Review documentation and implement the following:

- [Declared Age Range API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/declaredagerange/)
- [Significant Change API under PermissionKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/PermissionKit/SignificantAppUpdateTopic)
- [New age rating property type in StoreKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/ageRatingCode)
- [App Store server notification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreservernotifications/notificationtype)

2.  Use Apple’s sandbox testing environment to validate that the APIs have been implemented correctly.

For the most up-to-date requirements and API references, see:

## Upcoming Currency Change in Bulgaria

September 25, 2025

Beginning January 1, 2026, Bulgaria will adopt the euro (EUR) as its official currency, replacing the Bulgarian lev (BGN). As a result, the currency for App Store purchases and subscriptions in Bulgaria will change from BGN to EUR on this date. Your proceeds from sales in Bulgaria will also change to euros.

If you’ve selected the lev as the currency for your bank in App Store Connect, it will automatically change to the euro on December 1, 2025. If you’ve selected a different currency for your bank account, you’ll continue to be paid in that currency. The statutory fixed conversion rate is 1.95583 BGN = 1 EUR as established on July 8, 2025, by the Council of the European Union in connection with the introduction of the euro as the official currency in the Republic of Bulgaria.

- **Auto-renewing subscriptions, manually priced apps and In‑App Purchases, or apps and In‑App Purchases with Bulgaria as the base storefront:** Pricing on the Bulgaria storefront will be updated to a euro price based on the statutory conversion rate and rounded down to a supported euro price point. If you’ve chosen Bulgaria as the base storefront for your app or In‑App Purchases, pricing in other storefronts may be updated to maintain equalization with the new euro price.

&nbsp;

- **Other apps or In‑App Purchases:** Pricing on the Bulgaria storefront will be converted to the euro using the standard equalization for euro markets where App Store sales are subject to value‑added tax (VAT).

## Upcoming changes to offers and trials for subscriptions in South Korea

February 14, 2025

Starting February 14, 2025, new regulatory requirements in South Korea will apply to all apps with offers and trials for auto-renewing subscriptions.

To comply, if you offer trials or offers for auto-renewing subscriptions to your app or game, additional consent must be obtained for your trial or offer after the initial transaction. The App Store will help to get consent by informing the affected subscribers with an email, push notification, and in-app price consent sheet, and asking your subscribers to agree to the new price.

This additional consent must be obtained from customers within 30 days from the payment or conversion date for:

- Free to paid trials
- Discounted subscription offers to standard-price subscriptions

Apps that do not offer a free trial or discounted offer before a subscription converts to the regular price are not affected.

## Tax and price updates for apps, In-App Purchases, and subscriptions

February 6, 2025

The App Store is designed to make it easy to sell your digital goods and services globally, with support for 44 currencies across 175 storefronts.

From time to time, we may need to adjust prices or your proceeds due to changes in tax regulations or foreign exchange rates. These adjustments are made using publicly available exchange rate information from financial data providers to help make sure prices for apps and In-App Purchases stay consistent across all storefronts.

### Tax and pricing updates for February

**As of February 6:**

Your proceeds from the sale of eligible apps and In‑App Purchases have been modified in:

- Azerbaijan: value-added tax (VAT) introduction of 18%
- Peru: VAT introduction of 18%
- Slovakia: Standard VAT rate increase from 20% to 23%
- Slovakia: Reduced VAT rate introduction of 5% for ebooks
- Estonia: Reduced VAT rate increase from 5% to 9% for news publications, magazines, and other periodicals
- Finland: Reduced VAT rate increase from 10% to 14% for ebooks

Exhibit B of the Paid Applications Agreement has been updated to indicate that Apple collects and remits applicable taxes in Azerbaijan and Peru.¹

**As of February 24:**

Pricing for apps and In-App Purchases will be updated for the Azerbaijan and Peru storefronts if you haven’t selected one of these as the base for your app or In‑App Purchase.² These updates also consider VAT introductions listed in the tax updates section above.

If you’ve selected the Azerbaijan or Peru storefront as the base for your app or In-App Purchase, prices won’t change. On other storefronts, prices will be updated to maintain equalization with your chosen base price.

Prices won’t change in any region if your In‑App Purchase is an auto‑renewable subscription. Prices also won’t change on the storefronts where you manually manage prices instead of using the automated equalized prices.

The Pricing and Availability section of Apps has been updated in [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/login) to display these upcoming price changes. As always, you can change the prices of your apps, In‑App Purchases, and auto‑renewable subscriptions at any time.

## Reminder: Upcoming Changes to the App Store Receipt Signing Intermediate Certificate

January 16, 2025

As part of ongoing efforts to improve security and privacy on Apple platforms, the App Store receipt signing intermediate certificate is being updated to use the SHA-256 cryptographic algorithm. This certificate is used to sign App Store receipts, which are the proof of purchase for apps and In-App Purchases.

This update is being completed in [multiple phases](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=smofnyhj) and some existing apps on the App Store may be impacted by the next update, depending on how they verify receipts.

Starting **January 24, 2025**, if your app performs on-device receipt validation and doesn’t support the SHA-256 algorithm, your app will fail to validate the receipt. If your app prevents customers from accessing the app or premium content when receipt validation fails, your customers may lose access to their content.

If your app performs on-device receipt validation, update your app to support certificates that use the SHA-256 algorithm; alternatively, use the [AppTransaction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/apptransaction) and [Transaction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction) APIs to verify App Store transactions.

For more details, view [TN3138: Handling App Store receipt signing certificate changes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3138-handling-app-store-receipt-signing-certificate-changes).

## Algorithm changes to server connections for Apple Pay on the Web

January 9, 2025

Starting next month, Apple will change the supported algorithms that secure server connections for Apple Pay on the Web. In order to maintain uninterrupted service, you’ll need to ensure that your production servers support one or more of the [designated six ciphers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apple_pay_on_the_web/setting_up_your_server#overview) before **February 4, 2025.**

These algorithm changes will affect any secure connection you’ve established as part of your Apple Pay integration, including the following touchpoints:

- [Requesting an Apple Pay payment session](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apple_pay_on_the_web/apple_pay_js_api/requesting_an_apple_pay_payment_session) (Apple Pay on the Web only)
- [Renewing your domain verification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apple_pay_on_the_web/maintaining_your_environment#3179140) (Apple Pay on the Web only)
- [Receiving and handling merchant token notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaymerchanttokenmanagementapi/receiving-and-handling-merchant-token-notifications) for recurring, deferred, and automatic-reload transactions (Apple Pay on the Web and in app)
- Creating and updating [Wallet Orders](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletorders) (Apple Pay on the Web and in app)
- Managing merchant onboarding via the [Apple Pay Web Merchant Registration API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applepaywebmerchantregistrationapi) (payment service provider (PSP) and e-commerce platforms only)
